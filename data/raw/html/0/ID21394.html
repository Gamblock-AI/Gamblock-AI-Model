If you’ve used NotebookLM to generate slides, there’s good news: as of February 18, 2026, NotebookLM now supports slide-level editing and PPTX export. Part 1. How to Download NotebookLM Slides to PowerPoint - Official New Feature

On February 18, 2026, NotebookLM introduced slide-level editing and PPTX export support. Instead of regenerating an entire deck when making changes, users can now request modifications to a specific slide using AI instructions (for example, “fix slide 3”). Multiple edits can also be layered before regeneration, which improves workflow flexibility. In addition, NotebookLM now allows exporting slides as a PPTX file, not just PDF.

However, after in-depth testing, several important limitations remain:

  • Edits are still AI-generated rather than true element-level modifications
  • When modifying a slide, NotebookLM does not reference the original uploaded source materials
  • You cannot add or delete slides
  • Daily deck regeneration appears to have usage limits (community users report around 10–20 deck regenerations per day)
  • Layout and visual details may shift during regeneration

The following are the detailed steps.

Step-by-Step: Edit and Download NotebookLM Slides to PowerPoint

  • Access Revision Mode: Open your NotebookLM slide, navigate to the Studio panel, select the slide deck, and click the Revise (pencil) icon.
  • Enter Prompts: Provide clear, specific instructions for the updates (e.g., "switch the theme to blue", "Center the image on the page", or "Make the title shorter").
  • Batch Revisions: Submit multiple edits in one go; they will appear collectively under Pending Changes.
  • Regenerate Deck: Click Generate new deck to produce an updated version reflecting your requested revisions.
  • Download NotebookLM Slides: Click on the three dots menu (...) on the slide viewer and select Download PowerPoint (.pptx).
My Experience:

Editing slides in NotebookLM still relies a lot on the chat interface. Even small changes, like updating three slides, can take quite some time — sometimes up to an hour for longer decks. This works for gradual AI-assisted edits, but it can be slow when you need quick results. For faster and more precise editing, I often turn to OCR-based NotebookLM PDF editors to freely make manual adjustments to text, layouts, and design.

Part 3. How to Convert NotebookLM PDF to Editable PPT with Canva

When most users look for ways to convert NotebookLM PDF slides to editable PowerPoint, Canva is often the first choice. It’s familiar, easy to use, and can quickly import a PDF. You simply upload the NotebookLM PDF, and Canva will turn each page into a separate slide. For a quick visual presentation, this is convenient, especially if your slides are mostly images or simple layouts.

However, in practice, Canva has important limitations. NotebookLM exports slides as high-resolution layered images, not as editable text objects. While Canva can import the PDF, most of the text and elements remain unselectable.

Advanced features like Magic Grab or Magic Erase allow you to remove original elements and type over them, but this requires a Pro subscription and still doesn’t fully restore editable text. Fonts used in NotebookLM slides may not exist in Canva or PowerPoint, causing automatic replacements that can misalign text boxes or make slides look inconsistent.

My Own Experience Using Canva:

I’ve used Canva Pro for years and recently tried converting NotebookLM PDFs. Uploading was easy, and each page became a slide, but most text wasn’t editable. I spent an hour using Magic Grab and Magic Erase, and some text still needed retyping. Fonts were replaced automatically, requiring adjustments to spacing and alignment. Overall, it works, but it’s slow and far from seamless.

Steps to Convert NotebookLM PDF to Editable PPT in Canva:

  1. Step 1: Upload the PDF: Open Canva, click Upload, and select your NotebookLM PDF. Canva will convert each page into a slide automatically.
  1. Step 2: Make Text Editable (Magic Grab): If the text imports as an image, select a slide, click Edit (or right-click), go to Magic Studio, and choose Grab Text. This converts image text into editable layers.
  1. Step 3: Edit and Customize: Adjust, rearrange, or reformat text and elements on each slide as needed.
  2. Step 4: Download as PowerPoint: Click Share → Download, and choose Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx) as the file type.

Best Use: Canva is suitable for creating visual mockups or simple presentations, but it is not reliable for producing fully editable NotebookLM slides.

Part 4. Online Methods to Convert NotebookLM PDF to PPT (With Limits)

Some users try online tools to convert NotebookLM PDF slides to editable PowerPoint, but these methods have clear limitations:

  • Nano Banana : Generates visually appealing slides, but all text remains part of images. Editing content directly is impossible.
  • Python / Gemini API scripts: Can extract slide content, but setup is complex and time-consuming, unsuitable for most non-developers.
  • Free online converters (e.g., DeckEdit): Can process NotebookLM PDFs, but text often merges into images, charts may blur, and formatting may break. Privacy is also a concern since files are uploaded online.
  • Codia AI NoteSlide: It uses AI to turn NotebookLM PDFs into fully editable slides with real text and layouts, but the free trial allows only one conversion and complex designs may still need minor manual fixes.

Part 5. Tips for Generating Slides with NotebookLM

Generating slides in NotebookLM can save a lot of time, but planning ahead makes them easier to convert into editable PowerPoint later. Here are practical tips based on real experience:

  • Limit fonts and use common styles: NotebookLM sometimes uses artistic or unusual fonts. Stick to standard fonts to reduce text replacement issues when using OCR tools like PDNob.
  • Simplify backgrounds: Slides with gradients, semi-transparent shadows, or busy images may look great but can confuse OCR. Using simpler backgrounds ensures text is recognized accurately.
  • Keep charts and graphics simple: Complex charts with dense data, multiple axes, or heavy effects may be split into images plus scattered text after OCR. Simplifying visuals improves conversion quality.
  • Separate key sections clearly: Headings, main points, and data should be distinct. This helps OCR tools detect text boxes correctly and reduces manual adjustments in PowerPoint.
  • Preview and export in smaller batches: If your NotebookLM PDF has dozens of slides, exporting in smaller batches helps catch layout or font issues early.

FAQs

Is NotebookLM free to use?

Yes, NotebookLM is free for basic use with any Google account. It also offers a paid Plus version via Google’s AI Premium plan, which provides higher limits and advanced features. The free version works well for most personal research and note-taking, while the paid tier is designed for power users needing more notebooks, sources, and customization.

Can NotebookLM make a PowerPoint presentation?

Yes, NotebookLM now allows exporting slides as PPTX and supports in-app editing, but edits rely on the chat interface and exported PPTX files remain image-based. For fully editable slides, a tool like PDNob PDF Editor is recommended.

How to remove NotebookLM watermark from PDF?

You can remove the NotebookLM watermark from a PDF by using AI-powered online removers, editing in PDNob, or converting the file to an editable format.

Can Canva Pro fully edit NotebookLM slides?

Not reliably. Some text and elements remain uneditable due to layered image structures.

Why does the layout get messed up when converting NotebookLM PDF to PowerPoint?

NotebookLM exports slides as image-based PDFs, so text boxes, charts, and other elements aren’t recognized as separate objects. When converting to PowerPoint, this can cause overlapping text, misaligned layouts, or shifted images. Using a high-accuracy OCR-based PDF editor can help extract editable text and preserve layout, especially if you focus on text-heavy slides or simplify complex designs.

Final Thoughts

With the February update, NotebookLM now allows slide-level edits and PPTX export, but changes still rely on the chat interface and exported PPTX files remain partially uneditable. Free tools, Canva, or online converters often leave text uneditable or layouts misaligned, which can cost more time than expected.

The most reliable way is to plan your slides carefully—use standard fonts, simple backgrounds, and clear charts—so any OCR-based tool can handle them efficiently. In my experience, using PDNob PDF Editor for final conversion saves time and preserves editable text boxes, while keeping your files secure on your own computer.
