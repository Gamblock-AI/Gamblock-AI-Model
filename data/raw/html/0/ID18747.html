Yellowtail, a fish known as a "success fish," changes its name as it grows.
In Toyama, it's called tsubaiso, fukuragii, gandou...
Then, in the winter of its third year, it becomes buri.
Buri, which have grown strong and brave in the rough waves, are known as the "king of Toyama Bay."
Buri have also long been cherished as a lucky charm,
and have given rise to a unique food culture and customs.
This product, "Yellowtail Kamaboko," is crafted with the utmost care by artisans who cherish this traditional culture.
Please enjoy it.

  • In early winter, Toyama Bay can experience intense thunderstorms and sudden rough seas. This storm, known as "Buriokoshi," signals the start of winter yellowtail fishing for fishermen. The yellowtail that enter Toyama Bay during the storms are firm and especially fatty, making them highly valued for their delicious flavor.
  • The Hikotsu Kaido, connecting Etchu (Toyama) and Hida (Gifu), was once also known as the "Buri Kaido," and yellowtail caught in Toyama Bay was salted and transported to Takayama in Hida. For the people of this mountainous region, yellowtail was a valuable commodity and an essential New Year's treat. From Takayama, the yellowtail was then transported to Shinshu.
  • In Toyama, the custom of "buri-seigou," in which a whole yellowtail is given to a daughter's new husband at the end of the year, remains. The larger the yellowtail given, the better, as it is said to bring prosperity to the bride's family. It is also customary for the bride's family to return half of the gift to her parents.
  • Toyama is blessed with fresh seafood and is home to a unique kamaboko culture. Within the prefecture, the custom of cutting up celebratory kamaboko and distributing it to neighbors and relatives, "sharing" joy and happiness, remains.

We want to preserve Toyama's traditional food culture and pass it on to the younger generation. With this in mind, local businesses and creators have teamed up to launch the "Toyama Yellowtail Kama Project." "Shusse-uo no Yellowtail Kama" is our original product, combining two of Toyama's signature food cultures: yellowtail and decorative kamaboko. We paid particular attention to the design and packaging of the kamaboko, and the product went on sale on April 13, 2013, three years after its conception. We hope you enjoy Toyama's traditional food culture in a variety of settings.
