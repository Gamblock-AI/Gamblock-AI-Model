    Viant DSP FAQ

    What is DSP Advertising?

    Expand

    A demand-side platform (DSP) is a technology platform used in digital advertising to manage and optimize the buying process of ad inventory across multiple ad exchanges and supply sources. It provides advertisers and agencies with a centralized system to purchase and manage digital ad placements in real-time auctions. DSPs use sophisticated algorithms and data-driven insights to help advertisers target specific audiences, optimize ad performance, and achieve their campaign goals efficiently. DSP Advertising refers to the practice of using a DSP to execute programmatic ad buying, where advertisers can leverage audience data, targeting options, and real-time bidding capabilities to deliver relevant and targeted ads to the right users, at the right time, and in the most cost-effective manner. DSP Advertising empowers advertisers to reach their desired audiences across a wide range of websites, apps and other digital channels, while providing them with comprehensive control, insights, and flexibility in their ad campaigns.

    What are some examples of DSP Advertising?

    Expand

    There are several examples of demand-side platform (DSP) advertising that showcase its versatility and effectiveness in reaching target audiences. For instance, a retail brand may use a DSP to run a programmatic ad campaign targeting consumers who have shown interest in similar products or have visited their website recently. The DSP enables the brand to bid on ad placements in real-time auctions, ensuring that the ads are delivered to the right users across various traditional ad channels, like Display and Mobile, and emerging channels like Connected TV, Streaming Audio, Digital Out-of-Home and more. Similarly, a travel company could leverage a DSP to target specific demographics and geographical locations, delivering personalized ads to potential travelers who are more likely to engage with their offerings. Additionally, a financial institution could use a DSP to reach out to individuals who have displayed an interest in financial planning or investment-related content, allowing the institution to promote its services effectively. In all these cases, DSP advertising offers precise targeting, real-time optimization, and comprehensive insights capabilities to drive successful ad campaigns.

    Why are Demand Side Platforms important?

    Expand

    Demand-side platforms (DSPs) are important in the world of digital advertising for several reasons. Firstly, DSPs provide advertisers with access to a vast ecosystem of ad inventory across multiple publishers, ad exchanges, and supply sources. This allows advertisers to reach their target audiences at scale and with precision. Secondly, DSPs utilize sophisticated algorithms and data-driven insights to optimize ad campaigns in real-time, ensuring that ad impressions are served to the most relevant users, thereby maximizing the effectiveness of advertising budgets. Lastly, DSPs provide advertisers with comprehensive control, transparency, and reporting capabilities, enabling them to track and evaluate campaign performance, make data-driven decisions, and continually refine their advertising strategies. Overall, DSPs play a crucial role in enabling efficient and targeted programmatic advertising, driving better audience reach, engagement, and return on investment for advertisers.

    What’s the difference between DSP and SSP?

    Expand

    The main difference between a demand-side platform (DSP) and a supply-side platform (SSP) lies in their respective roles and perspectives within the programmatic advertising ecosystem. DSPs are used by advertisers and agencies to purchase and manage ad inventory, targeting specific audiences and optimizing ad performance. On the other hand, SSPs are utilized by publishers to manage and sell their ad inventory, connecting with multiple demand sources including DSPs to maximize revenue. While DSPs focus on buying ad placements, SSPs concentrate on selling ad placements, creating a seamless connection between supply and demand sides of the programmatic advertising marketplace.

    How do demand-side platforms work?

    Expand

    A demand-side platform (DSP) is a software service that allows advertisers to buy digital ad inventory across multiple exchanges in real time. Using automated bidding, DSPs help advertisers target specific audiences based on data signals like demographics, behaviors and device usage. This helps to ensure ads are shown to the right people at the right time, optimizing performance and budget efficiency.

    What is real-time bidding (RTB), and how does it work in a DSP?

    Expand

    Real-time bidding (RTB) is an automated process where digital ad inventory is bought and sold in milliseconds through an auction. When a user visits a website or app, a bid request is sent, and DSPs analyze the available data to determine if and how much to bid. The highest bidder wins, and their ad is instantly displayed, helping to ensure cost-efficient and highly targeted ad placements.

    How does Viant measure offline conversions or in-store visits?

    Expand

    Viant measures offline conversions and in-store visits by leveraging its proprietary Household ID™ technology and a robust identity graph. By connecting digital ad exposure to real-world locations and validated visitation data from partners like SafeGraph and Veraset, Viant can provide detailed "Foot Traffic Attribution" reports. Additionally, the platform allows advertisers to ingest their own first-party data—such as email lists or transaction records—which are matched against the Household ID to provide closed-loop reporting. To ensure accuracy, Viant uses a "ghost bidding" methodology to create control groups, enabling marketers to see the incremental lift and true ROI of their digital campaigns on physical retail sales.

    Expand

    With the launch of ViantAI, the timeline for taking a campaign live has been reduced from days to moments. Once the plan is generated, it can be seamlessly activated within the Viant DSP with the push of a button. This automation streamlines the transition from strategy to execution, allowing advertisers to launch high-impact omnichannel campaigns almost instantly while still maintaining full control over creative approvals and final optimizations.

    What role does artificial intelligence play in DSPs?

    Expand

    Artificial Intelligence serves as the central intelligence layer of a DSP, automating complex tasks that would be impossible to manage manually at scale. Within Viant’s platform, AI handles real-time bid optimization by analyzing millions of impressions per second to determine the ideal price for meeting specific KPIs like ROAS or eCPM. Beyond bidding, AI plays a critical role in media planning, fraud detection, and predictive audience segmentation, using machine learning to identify the users most likely to convert. By managing everything from frequency capping across devices to autonomous budget reallocation, AI allows marketers to shift their focus from tactical execution to high-level strategy, ensuring every dollar is spent on the most effective impressions.
