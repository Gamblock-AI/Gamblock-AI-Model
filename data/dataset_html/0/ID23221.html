Update: We (VillageSQL and Percona) met with Oracle on March 5th and presented the letter. While they have declined to be involved, they reiterated their commitment to transparency and community engagement. We will continue to maintain open conversations and encourage participation in the public discussion forums they are hosting.

Subject: Invitation to Discuss the Future of the MySQL Ecosystem

We, the undersigned, invite Oracle to join us in a conversation about establishing a non-profit Foundation to support the MySQL community.

We explicitly recognize Oracle as the owner of the MySQL software and trademark. We also see a number of pressing challenges facing the MySQL ecosystem today, and we believe a Foundation is the best way to tackle them head-on.

The Challenges MySQL Faces Today

1. Declining Popularity and Market Share MySQL is losing ground. PostgreSQL has become the default choice for many new projects and younger developers, thanks to its active community, rich feature set (especially extensions), and strong brand momentum. Attracting new MySQL deployments under the current model has become extremely difficult.

2. Lack of Transparency and Trust Development has largely happened behind closed doors through private code drops, with limited visibility into the roadmap or decision-making. Security-related bugs aren't publicly tracked, leaving users unable to verify whether known issues affect them. Despite new leadership signaling a more open approach, years of prioritizing proprietary enterprise features and HeatWave over the community edition have left deep skepticism.

3. Barriers to Community Contribution While it's technically possible to submit code, getting contributions reviewed and merged has been described as opaque and frustrating. The process feels more like a suggestion box than a genuine collaboration.

4. Technical Gaps MySQL still lacks features that are now table stakes for modern workloads - native vector search being a key example for AI use cases. The perception is that resources were shifted away from the open-source project to focus on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, leaving the community edition in something close to maintenance mode.

5. An Aging and Fragmented Community The MySQL community is getting older, and we're not doing enough to bring in new developers, students, and professionals. At the same time, the lack of a single vendor-neutral voice has led to fragmentation across forks and proprietary cloud extensions.

To address these systemic issues, we propose the formation of a vendor-neutral, non-profit Foundation. This entity would serve as a shared home for the ecosystem, independent of any single company's commercial interests. A Foundation isn't just paperwork; it is a practical, proven structure to fix the issues above.

How a Foundation Addresses These Challenges

Removing Contribution Barriers A Foundation creates a vendor-neutral governance model where contributions are evaluated on merit, not filtered through one company's internal priorities. This opens the door for major tech companies that want to participate but currently face too many barriers. Contributors can innovate without waiting for permission.

Rebuilding Transparency and Trust A Foundation can serve as a neutral steward for shared community assets - including a public bug database and a transparency log for security patches. This gives the community facts instead of assumptions.

Unifying the Ecosystem Using a trade association model (similar to OpenELA), the Foundation gives competitors a place to collaborate on the core standard while still competing on their own services. This creates a single center of gravity for MySQL, reducing fragmentation and ensuring compatibility.

Driving Adoption and Advocacy Today, no one promotes MySQL itself - vendors only promote their own products. A Foundation changes that by pooling resources to promote the platform as a whole:

  • A central "Everything MySQL" hub listing all distributions, cloud providers, and tools
  • Free training, certification paths, and university partnerships to engage the next generation
  • Vendor-neutral conferences and local meetups that aren't tied to any single company's marketing agenda

Proposed Strategic Dialogue

We propose a strategic dialogue around establishing a sustainable, vendor-neutral foundation for the MySQL community, focused on driving adoption and technical collaboration. We've identified three governance models to frame this discussion, depending on Oracle's preferred level of involvement.

Discussion Topic 1: Oracle-Led Governance (OpenELA Model) This model has Oracle take the lead in establishing the trade association, following the approach and structure that worked for OpenELA.

  • How it works: Oracle forms the organization and invites other industry leaders to join as founding members.
  • Why it works for Oracle: Oracle keeps control of the strategic direction and ecosystem vision while distributing the day-to-day work of ecosystem maintenance across partners.
  • Speed to launch: This approach builds on existing legal and operational frameworks from OpenELA, keeping startup costs and timelines low.

Discussion Topic 2: Collaborative Partnership and Licensing Model This model has the broader industry establish the foundation, with Oracle participating as a principal board member and strategic partner.

  • How it works: Community and industry partners handle logistics and administration, while Oracle holds a governance seat.
  • Trademark component: A key part of this discussion is negotiating an agreement that allows the Foundation to use the "MySQL" trademark specifically for non-profit advocacy and evangelism.
  • Why it works for Oracle: The community drives platform promotion and operations; Oracle's trademark rights are fully preserved without requiring day-to-day management involvement.

Discussion Topic 3: Independent Ecosystem Development This model has the community independently organize a trade association dedicated to advocacy and evangelism.

  • How it works: Industry partners form a separate legal entity that manages events, resource repositories, and adoption efforts.
  • Focus: This entity operates independently and provides a mechanism for accountability around commitments to the MySQL roadmap.
  • Oracle's Participation: We explicitly invite Oracle to participate in this initiative in whatever capacity they find suitable.

Should these models not align with Oracle's strategic vision, we welcome Oracle's alternative proposals for how we can collectively secure the future of the ecosystem.

Timeline

To maintain community momentum and continuity of support, we'd like to reach alignment on a direction by the end of March.

  • We're prepared to align our resources with whichever governance model best fits Oracle's current roadmap.
  • We invite Oracle to work with us to determine the best path forward.
