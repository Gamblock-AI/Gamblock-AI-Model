Introducing OIN 2.0

Meeting today’s changing landscape – Preparing for tomorrow’s challenges.

Open Source technologies are advancing faster than ever & patent litigation is escalating with defense costs averaging $4 million per case & over $5 million when claims exceed $25 million.

It’s time for OIN’s next chapter.

Since 2005, OIN has delivered protection by building the world’s largest & only patent defense community with 4,100+ members, supported by the original $160 million investment from our founding members

Now, 20 years later, OIN is evolving. To ensure we can sustain and expand protections for Open Source Software for decades to come, we’re introducing OIN 2.0 — a new model built on shared responsibility.

Chairman of the Board & CEO | Berkshire Hathaway

By joining OIN 2.0, you ensure continued protection for your Open Source technologies & the broader ecosystem.

Tiered Participation fees directly fund:

  • Expanding Linux System coverage, including emerging technologies

  • Adding more participants in the OIN cross-license & creating a stronger, more resilient community

  • Continued use of OIN’s resources to combat patent threats as they arise

“OIN 2.0 is a continuation of OIN’s long standing commitment to protect Open Source Software (OSS) from patent threats, modified to reflect today’s realities. Open Source technologies have grown exponentially beyond Linux, into new and exciting sectors, and clearly beyond what was originally envisioned in the early 2000s when OIN was created.

OIN 2.0 marks a transition and provides an opportunity for all who benefit from Open Source to contribute to OIN’s ongoing patent protection. OIN 2.0 fees will provide for OIN to update the scope of its patent protection coverage as OSS expands in the future, grow OIN’s network of participants to increase the breadth of its community, monitor patent threats to OSS, and support community members under patent threat in OSS with defensive strategies. The participation fee is structured so that large enterprises contribute more, medium-sized companies pay less, and small organizations remain free.”

Keith Bergelt

CEO | Open Invention Network
