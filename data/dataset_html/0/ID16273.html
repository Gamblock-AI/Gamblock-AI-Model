Schoolies - things to know before they go

DrinkWise, in partnership with the Australian Government’s Smartraveller has launched two short videos - one for parents and one for school-leavers - to help young people enjoy their Schoolies experience safely and mindfully. Featuring medical advice from Dr Andrew Rochford and messages from the families and friends of Bianca Jones and Holly Bowles who tragically died overseas after being served alcohol containing methanol, the videos share practical tips to help Schoolies have great time – and a safe one.

Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder Awareness Program

Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD) is 100% preventable. DrinkWise works to remind all Australians about the importance of not drinking alcohol when planning a pregnancy or when pregnant – and that it’s safest not to drink alcohol when breastfeeding.

Indigenous Alcohol Education Resources

DrinkWise has worked with 13YARN, the Richmond Football Club’s centre for Indigenous leadership - Korin Gamadji, Indigenous Clinical Psychologists, Indigenous artists and graphic designers and young mob to develop new Alcohol Education Videos, Student Resources and Teacher Resources featuring rapper, dancer, artist and actor Danzal Baker (or Baker Boy as he is better known) and AFL footballer Daniel Rioli.

Always respect, always DrinkWise - Gather Round

Gather Round is back and so is DrinkWise’s 2026 Always respect, always DrinkWise campaign. Supported by Mark Ricciuto, the South Australian Government, South Australia Police, Adelaide Metro, Adelaide Oval and support services including 1800RESPECT, No to Violence - Men’s Referral Service and 13YARN, it’s a reminder to enjoy the footy, drink in moderation if choosing to drink and respect everyone around you.

Never Have I Ever

The DrinkWise Never Have I Ever campaign is designed to prompt self-reflection, open up conversations about mental health and provide a clear pathway to advice and support for those who may be using alcohol to try to cope with stress and anxiety.

It's okay to say nay

Did you know that most Australian parents aren't supplying their underage teenagers with alcohol and the majority of underage teenagers in Australia are choosing not to drink alcohol. However, of those teenagers who are drinking alcohol, parents were the main source of alcohol supply.

Support Services

If you are concerned that you or someone you care about has a problem with alcohol or if your behaviours or actions are impacting on the safety of family or others, please ask for help. Here you can find useful links and phone numbers to get the advice and support you need.

  • National Alcohol and Other Drugs Hotline – 1800 250 015
  • 1800RESPECT – 1800 737 732
  • No to Violence Men’s Referral Service – 1300 766 491
  • 13 YARN – 13 92 76
